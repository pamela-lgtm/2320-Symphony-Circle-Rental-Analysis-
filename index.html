05 / Vacancy economics

The best rent is the rent that produces income

A reduction from $3,200 to $2,895 is $3,660 over a full 12 months. At $3,200, approximately 35 vacant days consume that entire premium. Thirty vacant days represent about $3,156 in forgone gross rent before ongoing ownership expenses.

Illustrations, not forecasts. Formula: monthly rent × 12 × (365 − vacancy days) ÷ 365. Assumes continuous paid occupancy after initial vacancy; excludes concessions, leasing/management fees, repairs, taxes, insurance, HOA, utilities and other costs. A fixed 365-day horizon makes the choices comparable.

Rent may not cover every ownership expense. Nevertheless, a qualified tenant paying a realistic market rent can offset part of those expenses while the owner retains the property. Vacancy produces no rental contribution. Compare net rental income after expenses and reserves—not asking rent alone—when deciding whether to hold or sell.

06 / Execution plan

A 21-day campaign with decisions built in

Day 0 • Reset to $2,895

Confirm showing condition, association rules and long-term lease terms. Refresh photos and listing facts. Make access easy, explain included lawn/internet and respond promptly.

Day 7–10 • Review $2,800

If qualified inquiries and completed showings remain weak, first fix access or listing errors, then reduce to $2,800 with owner approval. Do not wait for another month of inactivity.

Day 14–21 • Reassess $2,750

If no credible application or signed-lease progress emerges, refresh competition and consider $2,750. This is a contingency below the initial range, subject to new evidence and owner approval.

Measure weekly: inquiries, response time, completed/canceled showings, age/HOA eligibility questions, recurring objections, applications and reasons prospects decline. Few inquiries suggest price or visibility; inquiries without tours suggest access or terms; tours without applications suggest price, condition or mismatch. Investigate these signals rather than assuming price is the only issue.

Show-ready before launch: clean floors and carpets, neutral odors, working lights and appliances, comfortable temperature, tidy landscaping, clean patio/outdoor kitchen and accurate current photographs. Confirm internet and amenity benefits renters will actually receive. A strong presentation can attract a broader qualified audience; consistent screening, lease terms and ongoing management protect the tenancy. Presentation alone does not guarantee on-time payment or property care.

Coordinate the existing representation: The owner should ask the current Realtor for this written plan and weekly reporting, or resolve the existing listing agreement before appointing new representation. No duplicate listing or conflicting instructions are proposed. The issue is measurable leasing progress—not a conclusion about the current agent’s effort.
